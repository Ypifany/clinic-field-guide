A PUBLIC FIELD GUIDE FOR CLINIC WORK

Cybersecurity AI Risk and Governance Field Guide

By Cheryl Abram / Ypifany · First edition · October 2026

Help people understand what is happening in their digital space, make real choices, and recover when something goes wrong.

Use this guide during supervised work with a nonprofit, small business, or other willing organization. Begin with its situation and follow the sections as the work develops. Cloud systems and AI are included. You can also rehearse with the fictional food bank that appears throughout.

Choose tools that fit the people and task. Capture your work in drawings, notes, photographs, slides, or the clinic's existing systems. The methods here are options; use an equivalent that works for you.

Begin with the situation · Explore the tools · Ask about a clinic or mentor

The guide's definitions and working rules

Cybersecurity gives you the ability to sense what is happening in your digital space so you can make choices. Sensing includes discovering exposure before an event, noticing events as they happen, and understanding consequences afterward. Ask whose visibility is missing.

Safety means the person affected can sense what is happening, make a real choice, and recover if something goes wrong. All three must be present. A feature working as designed can still harm a person.

Risk is the chance that something changes, plus what that change would mean for you. Helpful and harmful changes both count. Name who gains and who carries each consequence.

Vulnerability in this guide means a place where change is easy. Whether the change matters is a separate risk question. Conventional technical usage also matters: NIST definitions commonly describe an exploitable or triggerable weakness. Keep the meaning being used clear.

Governance is how choices get made, who makes them, and how you check later whether they worked. Delegating a decision does not erase the choice to delegate.

A change agent can be a person, a storm, an attacker, or another cause of change. A threat is a change someone would not want; an opportunity is a change they would want. Always name whose perspective is being used.

AI use and human judgment

Use the AI and information approved for the engagement. AI may suggest, organize, draft, and help test. Check its contributions against evidence and the people involved. Explain what you accept, revise, or reject.

An AI agent uses AI to choose steps and use tools toward a goal. In this guide, reflexive responses are decided ahead of time and run within an agreed scope; reading, recommending, and small reversible actions may fit. Deliberate responses require a person to decide each time. Sending, deleting, spending, and granting access are deliberate.

Technical permission, permission for a purpose, and affected-person consent are separate. Renew authorization when the goal changes or the agent reaches new people or systems. If outside instructions try to redirect the agent, stop and alert; if behavior drifts from the task, pause and show the difference.

Limits before action

Agree on scope, methods, information handling, and who can stop. Separate a harmful outcome from an earlier warning that triggers review. State the response and what must be demonstrated before resuming. A reviewer needs a preview, enough understanding to question it, and a working way to stop.

Cloud systems

Find out which controls the client operates, which the provider supplies, and what evidence the clinic can obtain. Sharing permissions in a ready-made application and security settings in cloud infrastructure require different methods and skills. Use the provider's current shared responsibility guidance for the service being examined.

Nine operations functions

These functions operate together. Revisit them as understanding changes. See Governance and Risk Operations.

FunctionQuestion to keep in view
Set directionWhat matters, and to whom?
Maintain a boundaryWhat can enter, leave, or reach other people?
Sense conditionsWho can notice what is happening?
Interpret signalsWhat does the evidence support?
Regulate prioritiesWho decides what needs attention?
ActWhich actions require a decision each time?
Sustain functionWhat keeps the work and review possible?
Repair and recoverWhat can people restore?
Learn and adaptWhat changed, and which choice needs revisiting?

A mentor or supervisor reviews readiness and findings. Obtain the host organization's agreement before client work. People carrying consequences need a way to contribute their perspective.

TAB 01

Understand the Situation

What situation are people trying to understand, create, preserve, or change?

Begin with the situation and the current explanation of it. Identify the client, affected people, expected benefit, assumptions, and what the engagement can examine. Keep facts, interpretations, and unknowns distinguishable.

See it at the food bank

The fictional food bank asks for an AI assistant to answer donor questions faster. The director sees slow replies; a volunteer describes searching three cloud folders for the right answer. The team draws both accounts and keeps the cause of the delay open until it has evidence.

Try it with your client

Compare an owner's account of the problem with an affected person's account. Capture a situation statement and an initial frame in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • Rich pictures
    Sketch people, tools, pressures, and disagreements. Invite the client to correct the picture.
  • LEGO building and storytelling
    Build how work happens and explain the model. Formal LEGO SERIOUS PLAY is a facilitated option.
  • Excalidraw
    Draw a digital picture of the situation and return to it as understanding changes.

AI can help

Ask AI to compare approved descriptions of the situation, identify assumptions, and suggest questions that could reveal a different explanation.

Review and decide

Check every summary against what people actually said. Identify a missing perspective or an invented claim. Decide which questions to use, revise, or reject.

Keep in view
Keep observations, interpretations, and unanswered questions distinguishable.

TAB 02

Discover How Work Happens

What do people actually do with the tools available to them?

Use interviews, observation, and workflow walkthroughs to uncover informal AI use, shared accounts, shortcuts, and constraints. Let participants question the interview, decline a topic, and correct the record. Consider whether the interview setting lets them speak freely.

See it at the food bank

A volunteer walks through a donor reply using fictional records. The approved process says to use one folder, but the walkthrough reveals that staff also consult a personal spreadsheet. The team marks the workaround and asks what makes it necessary.

Try it with your client

Trace one real task, including its exceptions and workarounds. Capture a workflow map with observations and unanswered questions in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • Client walkthrough
    Ask someone to show one task using approved or sample information.
  • diagrams.net
    Draw the task as a storyboard, including exceptions and workarounds.
  • Simple Ethnography
    Explore an existing method for observing how work happens.

AI can help

Use approved notes to suggest a workflow map and follow-up questions. Ask AI to mark gaps instead of filling them in.

Review and decide

Compare the proposed sequence with the walkthrough. Confirm who performs each step and which details are still unknown. Let the participant correct the account.

Keep in view
Ask for the last actual example, then examine what made it possible.

TAB 03

Trace Decisions and Dependencies

Which earlier choices shape what the system does now?

Trace data, models, tools, vendors, policies, and human approvals. Identify who chose each dependency and what authority it carries. Separate the account owner, the person affected, and the people trusted with access. Follow consequences beyond the organization buying the system.

See it at the food bank

The draft reply moves from a cloud folder to an AI service and back to a staff member. The team maps the chosen services, the people who granted access, and the evidence it still needs about what the AI provider receives or retains.

Try it with your client

Trace a smart-lock event or an agent action back to access and delegation decisions. Capture a decision map and a record of dependencies and responsibilities in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • String and object map
    Connect objects representing people, services, and choices. Remove one connection and discuss the consequences.
  • diagrams.net
    Map data movement and the decisions that permit it.
  • Kumu
    Explore a richer relationship map when the client has many interacting people and services.

AI can help

Ask AI to propose a dependency map from approved descriptions and list questions about each connection.

Review and decide

Verify each named service, connection, and permission. Keep guesses visibly separate from confirmed dependencies. Check current provider information before making retention claims.

Keep in view
For each connection, ask what crosses it and who chose to allow that.

TAB 04

Inventory What Is Here

What capabilities, resources, and exposures are actually available?

Inventory devices, accounts, data, software, AI models, memory, tools, and allowed actions. Record access, logging, updates, and recovery arrangements. Include people's time, knowledge, relationships, and ability to act. Mark what is controlled directly, shared, or outside the engagement's control.

See it at the food bank

The team compares the food bank's account list with approved cloud sharing screens. It finds an old volunteer account still has access to a folder. Available records show the access grant; they do not yet establish whether the account was used.

Try it with your client

Review a bounded set of accounts and configurations with permission. Capture an asset and capability inventory with visibility gaps in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • Object or card sorting
    Group systems, data, and people by purpose, access, and control. Compare the grouping with actual settings.
  • Cloud access information
    Use the client's approved sharing or account screens. Google Drive guidance is one example.
  • Cloud responsibility guide
    Compare the provider's responsibilities with the customer's. Microsoft guidance is one example.

AI can help

Organize an approved list of accounts, services, data, and available controls. Ask AI to identify missing information.

Review and decide

Compare the list with actual settings and records. Check dates, coverage, and what the clinic can inspect. Confirm shared responsibility instead of assuming the provider handles everything.

Keep in view
Name what is controlled by the client, shared with a provider, or outside the clinic's view.

TAB 05

Examine Risk in Context

Where is change easy, and what could it mean for different people?

Model useful opportunities and unwanted changes, including harmful use by a technically authorized person. Examine relationships, technical boundaries, and changing circumstances. Use conventional threat methods alongside the guide's broader definitions, with any extensions clearly labeled.

See it at the food bank

Easier folder sharing could help volunteers answer donors sooner. It could also expose information to someone whose access is no longer appropriate. The team names both changes and asks who gains, who carries the consequence, and whose view is missing.

Try it with your client

Describe one change as seen by the owner and by the person carrying its consequences. Capture a scenario record covering change agents, vulnerabilities, consequences, and missing senses in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • OWASP Cornucopia
    Use the online application-security cards to prompt questions the team might overlook.
  • OWASP Threat Dragon
    Explore a sample model, then map an authorized system and possible threats.
  • What changes discussion
    Introduce a changed role, failed service, or new goal and discuss what it means to each person.

AI can help

Generate possible helpful and harmful changes from the approved situation. Use the suggestions to broaden inquiry.

Review and decide

Treat scenarios as possibilities until evidence supports them. Check feasibility, affected people, and useful opportunities. Do not let a threat score replace their perspective.

Keep in view
Ease of change is one question. What it means to someone is another.

TAB 06

Design Human and AI Collaboration

What work can be delegated while people retain meaningful judgment?

Match the person, task, AI capability, interaction, and operating context. Record the goal, tool limits, evidence requirements, reflexive and deliberate actions, and renewal conditions. Demonstrate the ability to review and stop.

See it at the food bank

The food bank permits an assistant to draft replies from approved sample material. A person reviews each recipient and message before sending. A rehearsal reveals that the reviewer cannot see the attachment, so the team keeps that action outside the assistant's scope until oversight works.

Try it with your client

Design an agent that reads records and recommends access changes, pausing before sending or granting access. Capture a collaboration agreement and an agent authorization record in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • Human and agent role-play
    Assign people to the assistant, reviewer, and affected person. Rehearse an unexpected request.
  • diagrams.net
    Draw who recommends, approves, acts, and can stop.
  • Improv Prototyping
    Use an existing facilitated method to act out and revise the workflow.

AI can help

Compare proposed divisions of work and draft a workflow that identifies human decisions, tool permissions, and stop conditions.

Review and decide

Rehearse the actual preview and stop controls. Check the person's time and understanding. In this guide, sending, deleting, spending, and granting access require a person to decide each time.

Keep in view
Human oversight needs a preview, enough understanding to question it, and a working way to stop.

TAB 07

Test Claims and Controls

What evidence answers the actual question?

Set context and anticipate errors before AI use. Review scope, depth, currency, system fit, source, completeness, and requirement match. Investigate doubts the checklist misses. Examine evidence, interview people, or test controls with permission. Keep a shared error log.

See it at the food bank

AI says access reviews happen every quarter because a policy requires them. The team separates that policy from evidence of completed reviews. A dated review record supports the work it documents; missing records leave questions to investigate.

Try it with your client

Distinguish a policy requiring account reviews, an email claiming completion, and records of the review. Capture a finding with evidence, uncertainty, and the next verification step in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • File activity and history
    Inspect what the service records and what those records can establish. Google Drive is one example.
  • Provider audit tools
    Examine available activity records within approved access. Microsoft Purview is one example.
  • AI testing resources
    Choose an optional evaluation tool when the scope and team skills support technical testing.

AI can help

Compare an exact claim with approved evidence. Ask AI to identify gaps, contrary evidence, and the next useful verification step.

Review and decide

Check scope, depth, currency, system fit, source, completeness, and requirement match. Trace the claim to the evidence yourself. Preserve uncertainty when the records cannot answer the question.

Keep in view
A requirement, a report of completion, and evidence of the work support different claims.

TAB 08

Put Governance into Practice

Who can choose, refuse, intervene, and revisit the decision?

Record decision authority, delegation, purpose, consent, and who carries consequences. Connect access changes to changing purposes and relationships. Specify signals, review dates, stop authority, notification, and recovery support. Include a route for the affected person when the account owner is the source of harm.

See it at the food bank

The former volunteer's access was granted for a role that has ended. The team asks who can review the grant, who could be affected by leaving it active, and how people can raise a concern. It drafts a rule with a review trigger and an actual person able to intervene.

Try it with your client

Revisit an access grant after its original reason no longer applies. Capture a decision record with review triggers and a tested intervention path in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • Minimum Specifications
    Explore an existing method for agreeing on essential rules and limits.
  • CISA scenarios
    Choose a relevant scenario and rehearse whether the rules work under pressure.
  • Decision map
    Reuse the team's drawing to show who can choose, refuse, pause, and authorize resumption.

AI can help

Draft options for decision authority, review triggers, notification, and recovery from approved findings.

Review and decide

Confirm that named people have the authority, access, time, and ability to act. Separate technical permission, permission for a purpose, and affected-person consent.

Keep in view
Every delegated choice needs a purpose, authority, and a way to revisit it.

TAB 09

Present Findings and Agree on Next Steps

Can the client and affected people understand the findings and make a real choice about what follows?

Use the work developed from Tab 1 onward. Present supported findings, uncertainty, options, and consequences. Review the recommendations with a supervisor and invite the client to correct the account. Agree on responsibilities, recovery support, follow-up, and closure of temporary access.

See it at the food bank

The team presents two options for folder access and AI drafting. The food bank chooses the smaller change because staff can maintain it now. The team names what that choice leaves unresolved and agrees who will review it after the next volunteer turnover.

Try it with your client

Present one finding and two feasible options. Ask the client to explain the consequence of each in their own words. Capture a usable account of the findings and agreed next steps in the client's preferred format in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • Existing slides or whiteboard
    Reuse the client's familiar tool and the diagrams already created.
  • 1-2-4-All
    Explore an existing way to gather views from more participants.
  • Role-swapping rehearsal
    Explain the recommendation as the client, staff member, or affected person, then identify what is unclear.

AI can help

Draft a plain-language explanation of verified findings and compare feasible options. Use approved information.

Review and decide

Remove unsupported certainty and promises. Check that the choices fit the client's resources. The team reviews the recommendation; the client makes the decisions assigned to it.

Keep in view
For each option, show the benefit, consequence, uncertainty, and next decision.

TAB 10

Evaluate and Revalue

What can people see now, and what should happen next?

Use the Decision Visibility Model to compare expectations with observations, examine the delta, inventory resources, and reframe. Assess preserved choice, meaningful recovery, detection, surprises, and useful opportunities with affected people and advocates. Test learning with an unfamiliar case.

See it at the food bank

In a fictional follow-up, staff can see sharing changes sooner and stop an AI draft before sending. They also report that account recovery instructions are still hard to use. The team records the visibility and choice gains, keeps the recovery gap open, and revisits the earlier decision.

Try it with your client

Choose whether to continue, stop, change, or observe, and explain the evidence. Capture an evaluation and a revised bet with observable signals in a format that suits the client.

Ways to explore it

  • What So What Now What
    Explore an existing method for reflecting on experience and deciding what follows.
  • CISA after-action resources
    Consult existing exercise review guidance when useful.
  • Revisit the rich picture
    Compare versions of the original drawing and mark what people can see now.

AI can help

Compare approved before-and-after observations, identify differences from expectations, and suggest follow-up questions.

Review and decide

Check the comparison with the client and affected people. Separate observation from explanation. Decide whether to continue, stop, change, or observe, using preserved choice and meaningful recovery as priorities.

Keep in view
Compare the original bet with what people can now sense, choose, and recover.

EXPRESS INTEREST

Organizations and mentors

Bring a clinic to your organization

If you are a nonprofit or small business, describe the situation you want help understanding, the systems involved, your timing, and who can support the work. Cloud and AI systems can be included when the engagement has suitable expertise and scope.

Ask about an organization engagement

On the Ypifany contact form, begin your message with “Organization clinic inquiry.” Include a general description; leave credentials and sensitive records out of the inquiry.

Work through a clinic with a mentor

Describe your experience, what you want to learn, availability, and whether you have an organization willing to host the work. The starting arrangement is that you find the organization and obtain its agreement. Mentoring can help you examine evidence, exercise judgment, and review the work.

Ask about mentoring

On the Ypifany contact form, begin your message with “Clinic mentoring inquiry.” An inquiry expresses interest; mentor availability, scope, and arrangements need discussion.

You can use this public guide independently of an inquiry. Teams with an existing supervisor can apply it within their own authorized engagement.

The contact page introduces several Ypifany services, including paid Clarity Sessions. Identify your request as a clinic or mentoring inquiry so the scope, availability, and arrangements can be discussed.
